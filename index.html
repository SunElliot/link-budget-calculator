What is a satellite link budget?

A link budget accounts for all the gains and losses a radio signal experiences from the transmitter, through the propagation path, to the receiver. It tells you whether a satellite link will close — i.e. whether the received signal is strong enough to be demodulated at your target bit error rate (BER) — and how much margin you have against fades and uncertainties.

The equations used by this calculator

EIRP = Ptx(dBW) + Gtx(dBi) − Ltx(dB)

Free space path loss (FSPL) = 32.44 + 20·log₁₀(dkm) + 20·log₁₀(fMHz) dB

G/T = Grx(dBi) − Lrx(dB) − 10·log₁₀(Tsys) dB/K

C/N₀ = EIRP − Lpath + G/T − k, where k = −228.6 dBW/K/Hz (Boltzmann)

Eb/N₀ = C/N₀ − 10·log₁₀(Rb)

Link margin = Eb/N₀received − Eb/N₀required − implementation loss

Typical LEO smallsat values

For a CubeSat S-band downlink you might use ~2 W transmit power, a 6 dBi patch antenna, a 3 m ground-station dish (~30 dBi at S-band), a slant range around 1500–2500 km, and a system noise temperature of 150–250 K. A healthy design keeps at least 3 dB of margin at the lowest elevation angle you plan to operate.
